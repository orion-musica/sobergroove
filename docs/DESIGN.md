# Design notes

Sober Groove is a recovery companion for musicians in the first weeks and months without alcohol. These are the rules every feature has to pass.

1. **No death, no game over.** The buddy can be sleepy or quiet, never sick or gone. Losing a pet after a lapse feeds shame, and shame feeds drinking.
2. **A slip is not a relapse.** Total sober days, waves ridden, lessons and the stage never go down. Only the current streak restarts.
3. **No judgement.** Warm, short, concrete words. Never "you failed" or "start over".
4. **No alcohol imagery.** No bottles, glasses or bars in art or text. The world is the rehearsal room and the stage.
5. **People before the device.** Now and then the buddy mentions people, support groups or a professional, lightly, as an interesting path and not an order. It must never become someone's only support.
6. **Small wins count.** Every wave ridden out and every lesson is its own success.
7. **Private and discreet.** Data stays on the device. To others it looks like a synth.

## The caretaker model

You are the buddy's caretaker and teacher. It grows along two axes: sober days decide its stage; lessons decide what it can play. Caring for someone else gives meaning and responsibility, and helping others supports one's own sobriety. Nothing is taken away if you do not teach: the buddy simply practises what it knows.

## The story

A musician kicked out of the band and the flat knocks on your door. We never say whose problem is "smaller": you are on the same road, a few steps further today. On a bad day the roles can turn and the buddy supports you. The buddy never leaves, not even after the main stage. A slip does not undo the story: "Still here, still on your couch."

## Craving waves

Cravings rise, peak and pass; the peak rarely lasts more than a few minutes, and many sources describe a wave of up to 20–30 minutes that weakens with each one ridden out. The SOS mode is built around that: a visible wave, slow breathing with the bass, a HALT check (hungry, angry, lonely, tired?) and something to do with the hands.

## Who it is for

Surveys and research describe higher alcohol use among musicians than the general population, linked to job insecurity, nightlife and the culture of free drinks. The three buddies, a bassist, a drummer and a DJ, cover live bands and the club scene.
